# Issue #6: Exa and Tavily search-provider research

Checked 5 October 2026. This is a documentation-based assessment for the [six daily German search queries in issue #6](https://github.com/rotimi-babalola/news-intelligenz/issues/6), initially restricted to `tagesschau.de` and `deutschlandfunk.de`. Search is meant to add relevant publications to RSS, preserve discovery provenance, and stop when a provider's free allowance is exhausted; the project's [agreed design](phase-1-design-notes.md) calls for measured additional coverage and resource usage. Prices and terms can change, so recheck them when registering.

## Short answer

**Yes, both can be evaluated, and both fit the stated six-query daily schedule on their advertised free allowances.** Their published Search API documentation supports the required domain restriction, date filtering, and result URLs. I found no exclusivity requirement in the reviewed provider terms that requires choosing only one. This is a technical and contractual reading of the linked terms, not a legal clearance for retaining or republishing provider output. [Exa Search API](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/search) · [Tavily Search API](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-reference/endpoint/search) · [Exa terms](https://exa.ai/assets/Exa_Labs_Terms_of_Service.pdf) · [Tavily platform terms](https://www.tavily.com/terms)

Use the **Search** endpoints for this experiment. Exa's separate Agent product and Tavily's Research endpoint do multi-step research and have materially different pricing; issue #6 needs candidate article URLs from fixed queries. ## Constraints to resolve before production use

- Avoid attaching a payment method or enabling pay-as-you-go during the free experiment. Exa says the free plan needs no payment method; Tavily distinguishes its free plan from pay-as-you-go charging beyond the plan limit. The collector must enforce its own per-provider budget and pause that provider before an overage. [Exa pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing) · [Tavily credits](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-credits) · [Project design](phase-1-design-notes.md)
- Exa's published terms broadly restrict copying or publishing information obtained through the service except where expressly permitted. The apparent interaction between that wording and ordinary API result storage should be clarified with Exa before retaining its result text or republishing it. Independently verify the publisher's rights before storing article text. [Exa terms, §4.2(a)](https://exa.ai/assets/Exa_Labs_Terms_of_Service.pdf) · [Project design](phase-1-design-notes.md)
- Tavily's platform terms restrict disclosing performance information or analysis about its service to third parties. Keep comparative benchmark results private unless Tavily authorizes disclosure; do not put provider-specific measured scores in a public repository. Its terms also limit use to the customer's internal business purposes while allowing integration with customer applications; the planned internal collector appears compatible, but a future public product deserves a separate terms review. [Tavily platform terms, §§2–3](https://www.tavily.com/terms)
- Neither provider's documentation proves accuracy, recall, or freshness on these domains. Tavily notes its published-date field is a best estimate and may represent an update. Treat both dates and relevance as evidence to verify against publisher pages and RSS. [Tavily Search reference](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-reference/endpoint/search) · [Exa Search reference](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/search)

## Paired result-quality check

1. Run the same six fixed German queries on both providers at the same daily time for seven days, with the same two allowed domains, a comparable 48-hour date window, and at most ten results per query. Use Exa `type: "auto", category: "news"` and Tavily `search_depth: "basic", topic: "news"`; disable generated answers and extra page-content retrieval. This costs at most 42 Search calls/provider for the week under one run/day, before retries. Both APIs support these controls, though their date semantics differ. [Exa Search reference](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/search) · [Tavily Search reference](https://docs.tavily.com/documentation/api-reference/endpoint/search)
2. Normalize the returned URLs, verify the publisher host, and compare against the configured RSS feed items over the same interval. Preserve provider, query, position, request time and reported usage in a **private** trial record; avoid copying full snippets into the public repo. [Project design](phase-1-design-notes.md) · [Tavily terms](https://www.tavily.com/terms) · [Exa terms](https://exa.ai/assets/Exa_Labs_Terms_of_Service.pdf)
3. Hand-check a balanced sample across all six subjects and both providers (at least 30 results per provider): Germany relevance, distinct article versus section/tag page, actual publication time, and whether RSS already contained it. Report precision in the checked sample, additional relevant URLs beyond RSS, unique contribution by provider, stale/invalid URLs, overlap, failures and credits/cost. Keep provider-specific measured scores private under Tavily's current terms. This is a proposed experiment, **not measured evidence**. [Project completion criteria](phase-1-design-notes.md) · [Tavily terms](https://www.tavily.com/terms)

**Decision rule:** use both during the bounded trial. Continue both only if the second provider finds enough additional relevant articles to justify its extra calls and operational work; otherwise choose the stronger one for the daily run. This is a project recommendation, not a provider claim. With the present free allowances, cost alone does not force an either/or decision.
